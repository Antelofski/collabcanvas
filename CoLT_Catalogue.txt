CoLT Categories
Discussion
Think-Pair-Share
Definition: Students think individually, discuss their responses with a partner, and then share with the whole class.
Advantage: It prepares students to participate more confidently and effectively in whole-class discussions.
Round Robin
Definition: Students take turns sharing ideas in a fixed order within a group.
Advantage: It structures brainstorming and ensures that every student has an opportunity to contribute.
Buzz Groups
Definition: Students discuss course-related questions informally in small groups.
Advantage: It generates many ideas quickly and prepares students for richer whole-class discussions.
Talking Chips
Definition: Students contribute a token each time they speak during a group discussion.
Advantage: It promotes balanced participation by encouraging quieter students to contribute and limiting dominant speakers.
Three-Step Interview
Definition: Students interview each other in pairs and then report what they learned to another pair.
Advantage: It helps students build connections and strengthen their listening and communication skills.
Critical Debate
Definition: Students prepare and defend a position that opposes their personal views on an issue.
Advantage: It develops critical thinking by encouraging students to examine alternative perspectives and question their assumptions.
Reciprocal Teaching
Note-Taking Pairs
Definition: Students compare and combine their individual notes to create an improved shared version.
Advantage: It helps students fill information gaps, correct inaccuracies, and improve their note-taking skills.
Learning Cell
Definition: Students take turns asking and answering questions they individually prepared about a reading or learning activity.
Advantage: It encourages active engagement with course content and deeper thinking through peer questioning.
Fishbowl
Definition: Students form inner and outer circles, with the inner group discussing while the outer group listens and observes.
Advantage: It allows students to observe and learn effective discussion practices and group interactions.
Role-Play
Definition: Students adopt different identities and act out a scenario.
Advantage: It engages students creatively and helps them learn through direct participation.
Jigsaw
Definition: Students develop expertise in a topic and then teach it to their peers.
Advantage: It motivates students to understand material deeply enough to explain it clearly to others.
Test-Taking Teams
Definition: Students study together, take a test individually, and then retake the test as a group.
Advantage: It helps students identify gaps in understanding while learning content and test-taking strategies from peers.
Problem-Solving
Think-Aloud Pair Problem-Solving (TAPPS)
Definition: Students work in pairs, with one explaining their problem-solving reasoning aloud while the other listens.
Advantage: It makes the reasoning process visible and helps students identify errors in logic or procedure.
Send-a-Problem
Definition: Groups solve a problem and pass it with their solution to another group, with the final group evaluating the accumulated solutions.
Advantage: It develops collaborative problem-solving skills and helps students compare and evaluate different solutions.
Case Study
Definition: Students analyze a written account of a real-world situation and propose a solution to its central dilemma.
Advantage: It connects abstract theories and principles to relevant, practical situations.
Structured Problem-Solving
Definition: Students follow a defined sequence of steps to identify, analyze, and solve a problem.
Advantage: It makes complex problems more manageable and develops an organized approach to solving them.
Analytic Teams
Definition: Students assume distinct analytical roles, such as summarizer or critic, to examine a reading, lecture, or video together.
Advantage: It helps students practice individual components of critical analysis before combining them into a more complete analysis.
Group Investigation
Definition: Students collaboratively plan, conduct, and report on an in-depth research project.
Advantage: It develops research skills while building detailed knowledge of a specific topic.
Graphic Organizer
Affinity Grouping
Definition: Students generate ideas, identify common themes, and organize the ideas into related groups.
Advantage: It helps students break down complex topics and classify their main components.
Group Grid
Definition: Students place given pieces of information into a grid according to specified categories.
Advantage: It clarifies conceptual categories and strengthens students’ classification skills.
Team Matrix
Definition: Students complete a chart showing whether related concepts possess particular defining features.
Advantage: It helps students distinguish between closely related concepts by comparing their key characteristics.
Sequence Chains
Definition: Students create a diagram showing an ordered series of events, actions, or decisions.
Advantage: It helps students understand processes, chronological order, and cause-and-effect relationships.
Word Webs
Definition: Students organize related ideas visually and connect them with lines or arrows to show relationships.
Advantage: It helps students identify and represent connections among concepts.
Writing
Dialogue Journals
Definition: Students record their thoughts in journals and exchange them with peers for comments and questions.
Advantage: It connects coursework to personal experience while encouraging thoughtful written interaction.
Round Table
Definition: Students take turns adding short written responses to a prompt before passing the paper to the next person.
Advantage: It provides informal writing practice and creates a shared written record of ideas.
Dyadic Essays
Definition: Students prepare essay questions and model answers, exchange questions with a partner, and compare their responses with the model answers.
Advantage: It helps students identify key learning points and practice developing and answering substantive questions.
Peer Editing
Definition: Students critically review a peer’s writing and provide constructive editorial feedback.
Advantage: It develops editing skills and helps students improve their work before submitting it for assessment.
Collaborative Writing
Definition: Students work together to produce a formal paper.
Advantage: It helps students learn and carry out the stages of writing more effectively.
Team Anthologies
Definition: Students compile course-related readings and add their own responses to the selected material.
Advantage: It gives students experience with the research process without requiring a formal research paper.
Paper Seminar
Definition: Students write and present an original paper, receive feedback from selected peers, and discuss its issues with the whole group.
Advantage: It supports in-depth discussion of research and provides focused feedback on individual students’ work.
Games
Team Scavenger Hunt
Definition: Students work in teams to locate items from a course-related list.
Advantage: It introduces students to important artifacts and concrete visual or physical examples of course concepts.
Quizo
Definition: Students answer questions to earn chips and place them on a board, aiming to cover five consecutive spaces.
Advantage: It provides an engaging way to introduce or review factual information.
Friendly Feud
Definition: Students generate multiple correct answers to a single prompt question.
Advantage: It helps students recognize that a question can have several valid answers with varying degrees of correctness.
Team Jeopardy
Definition: Students select categories and point values, receive an answer, and supply the corresponding question.
Advantage: It encourages students to approach familiar content from a different direction by constructing questions from answers.
Team Games Tournaments
Definition: Students study in mixed-ability teams and then compete with similarly skilled peers to earn points for their home teams.
Advantage: It provides a competitive group activity for assessing students’ mastery of specific course content.
