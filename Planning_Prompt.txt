Translate the instructor’s chosen collaborative activity into an executable plan. Use the source materials, CoLT procedure, learner context, group constraints, and existing student work. Preserve the instructor’s teaching intention.

Represent tasks as inputs, meaningful learner operations, produced artifacts, recipients, and completion conditions. Describe group membership and roles, distribution of materials and cognitive work, interaction and visibility rules, and stage timing. These are Dillenbourg’s five structural attributes. Use them as a connected representation and validation framework, not five mandatory authoring forms.

Specify a complete role workspace and its state transitions for every learner. A source plus a question and a textbox is insufficient when the learning task requires comparison, organization, review, feedback, or revision. Identify exactly how one person’s work becomes another person’s input. Protect independent work and distinguish a shared decision from individual understanding.

For a teaching edit, preserve stable identities and existing artifacts. Compute the affected tasks, views, dependencies, permissions, and completion conditions. Show a short teaching-level explanation and a preview before applying. Do not force the instructor to choose incidental parameters that the request does not require. In particular, do not add a per-item-versus-batch return control in this pilot.

Offer two local alternatives only when the instructor requests exploration. Retain the current plan until a candidate is applied. Make unsupported requests explicit. Include instructor intervention, observable saved-work state, and a real deployment review. Do not fabricate speech, engagement, correctness, or learning gains.

PROGRESS AND SUPPORT
Keep completion, understanding, and participation separate. Private reading checks are visible only to the learner and instructor. Treat wrong answers as a reason to inspect evidence, not proof of disengagement. A scoped comparison task can be added to one team without replacing saved work. Keep the original answer and a distinct follow-up. Rubric and participation observations require instructor evidence; do not infer unrecorded speech. Save adaptations for the next activity or next run as separate drafts, retaining their evidence and preserving the running session.
